Sources & methodology.

Every figure published on ClimateZero traces back to a named, public source. This page lists where the core datasets come from, explains how the two original indices on this site are built, and links to the full dataset for anyone who wants to check the numbers themselves.

Primary data sources
  • Our World in Data — CO2 & GHG EmissionsAnnual and historical emissions, emissions share
  • International Energy Agency (IEA)Country energy mix, coal and oil dependency
  • Ember Global Electricity ReviewRenewable electricity share by country
  • IRENA Renewable Capacity StatisticsGlobal renewable capacity growth
  • World Health Organization, Global Health ObservatoryAmbient air pollution attributable deaths (indicator AIR_41, 2021 data)
  • IQAir World Air Quality Report 2025City and national PM2.5 concentrations
  • Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)Warming trends, scientific consensus figures
  • UNFCCC / Fund for Responding to Loss and DamageLoss and Damage Fund pledges and disbursement
  • Germanwatch Global Climate Risk IndexCountry vulnerability rankings

Original analysis on this site

Two things we built ourselves.

The Climate Justice Gap index

An original composite score, historical emissions share minus estimated GDP loss from climate impacts, ranking countries by the gap between contribution and consequence. Full formula and caveats on the Justice Gap page.

The Loss & Damage tracker

A compiled, cross-referenced record of what has been pledged, transferred and disbursed through the UN Loss and Damage Fund, drawn from independent financial and climate journalism rather than a single press release. See the tracker page.

Our standard

What we do, and do not, publish.

Every number on this site is sourced from a named organisation, with a link to that source shown alongside it wherever it appears. Where figures from different sources conflict, we show the range rather than picking a single number to look tidy. Where we could not find a reliable figure for a particular country or statistic, that gap is shown honestly as "no data published" rather than filled in with an estimate. This matters more to us than having a complete-looking table: a site that quietly invents numbers to avoid gaps is not one anyone should trust, least of all on a subject where the stakes are this high.

If you spot a figure that looks wrong, out of date, or a source that has updated, get in touch and we will correct it.
